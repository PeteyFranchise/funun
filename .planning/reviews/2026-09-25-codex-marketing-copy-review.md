## Overclaims

1. **"First" does not survive scrutiny.** Prior art cited: [Songcraft](https://songcraft.io/)
   (real-time collaborative songwriting, comments, text and video chat),
   [Lyree](https://lyree.io/) (co-writers in the same song, lines marked as they land),
   [Soundtrap](https://www.soundtrap.com/content/product/online-daw-features). "Topline" narrows
   the category but not enough to be defensible without a dated market study.
2. **"Know who owns it"** confuses evidence with legal conclusion.
3. **"Every line remembers who wrote it"** promises granularity the product may not have.
4. **The privacy sentence is absolute** and must hold across APIs, exports, notifications, shared
   PDFs, workspace access and support tooling — and may be the wrong model for a split sheet all
   parties must review and sign.
5. **"Exports clean everywhere"** promises universal schema compatibility.
6. **"One-stop-licensed"** is stronger than "ready for one-stop clearance."
7. **"Our team starts putting it in front of supervisors"** creates an immediate service obligation.
8. **"Completeness and accuracy"** overstates AI contract review.
9. **"Lossless 24-bit capture"** must not ship before the deployed recorder reliably produces it.
10. **"Dedicated liaison"** may imply reserved capacity or exclusivity.
11. **"Decades of combined industry experience"** needs substantiation and reads generic.

## Inconsistencies

- **Writer's Room vs Room.** The core product and the team tier share a word. Codex suggests
  `Team` for the paid tier.
- **Line / section / bar / timecode** used interchangeably across the page.
- **"Song diary" vs Diary** — capitalise only if it is a named surface.
- **Registration language** — collaborators "auto-fill registrations" vs Release Report's correct
  "guidance and tracking." Standardise.
- **Sync language** — Antenna matches opportunities; The Crate is an admitted catalogue. One
  sentence should distinguish them.
- **Storage language** — two rows imply two balances.
- **Entourage** sounds like a fourth tier but means only volume pricing.
- **Tone** — Writer sounds human; Room introduces "audit surfaces", "assigned to your account",
  "build the gameplan", "priced per engagement".
- **Footer taxonomy** mixes product names and categories.

## Free tier allowance — recommendation

**Option A as the product model, D as the rollout discipline.**

- Unlimited songs.
- Compressed Funūn-recorded hums and rough takes do **not** draw down the paid allowance.
- Free gets a starter allowance for uploaded masters, stems and generated PCM/lossless —
  **2 GB a reasonable beta starting point**.
- Studio raises it and unlocks 24-bit capture.
- **Do not publish the number until an authoritative meter and enforcement experience exist.**

Takes should be *unmetered for upgrade purposes* while ordinary file-size, rate, abuse and
inactive-account controls still apply.

### Why not meter takes

~0.72 MB/min means ~47 hours of compressed capture to reach 2 GB. A three-minute 24-bit keeper is
~72× a three-minute compressed take. The sensible paid boundary is the transition from ideas to
durable, delivery-quality assets. Metering takes charges users for the exact behaviour the Writer's
Room exists to create. The counter-argument is abuse — which justifies quiet operational
safeguards, not a visible GB meter while humming.

### Egress matters more than the storage sticker price

Supabase: 100 GB storage with Pro, $0.021/GB-month beyond; separate 250 GB cached and uncached
egress quotas, then $0.03/GB cached and $0.09/GB uncached.
([billing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-on-supabase) ·
[egress](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/egress))

- 2 GB stored for a month ≈ **$0.042** past the shared allowance.
- 2 GB played ten times = 20 GB egress ≈ **$0.60 cached** / **$1.80 uncached** past the allowance.

Quotas are per organisation, not per member. Mitigations instead of a playback meter: compressed
preview derivatives for large masters, CDN caching and range requests, short-lived signed URLs, no
public hotlinking, rate-limit abnormal repeated downloads, measure egress by media class before
setting Studio's number. *"A user should not have to wonder whether pressing play again is spending
their allowance."*

### Interim copy, until enforcement ships

- Free: `Unlimited songs. Rough takes included.`
- Studio: `Lossless capture and more audio storage.`

Then, once enforced: `2 GB for masters, stems and lossless takes. Rough takes don't count against
that allowance.`

### The upgrade moment

Should feel like **the song has become worth keeping at full quality**, not like the demo pad
stopped working. Warn at thresholds, show the largest files, offer cleanup. At the limit: existing
audio stays playable/downloadable/deletable, ordinary compressed capture continues, only new
master/stem/lossless additions pause, nothing is silently deleted, and existing files are
grandfathered if enforcement arrives after users already exceed it.

## Build target — recommendation

**Option A: a route in the existing Next app.**

The page does not need React as its *interaction model* — the prototype proves carousel,
disclosures, audio controls and reveal effects work with ordinary HTML, CSS and small JS. It should
still live in Next because Next already owns the domain, deployment, auth handoff, metadata,
analytics boundary, security headers and design tokens.

Build most of it as **static server-rendered markup**; add client code only for carousel controls,
accessible popovers/dialogs, custom audio-player state, and a reveal observer if CSS is
insufficient. *"React becomes the delivery shell, not a reason to turn every section into a client
component."*

### Why not a separate build

Dropping React from the implementation does **not** erase option B's cost. A separate target still
means another deployment, domain/rewrite config, analytics, consent handling, headers, environment
config, preview workflow and a design system that drifts. The low-cost version of "plain HTML" is
not a second site — it is plain semantic markup rendered by the existing Next route.

The public root should render the marketing page instead of sending every logged-out visitor to
`/signin`.

### How much would adopting the 21st.dev stack save?

**Roughly 20–35% of the porting work** — Codex's stated inference, not a measured estimate. Saves
primitive setup, some keyboard/focus behaviour, variant plumbing, icon replacement, motion
scaffolding. Does **not** save deciding what the component should do in Funūn, rewriting content
and hierarchy, reskinning, responsive composition, product-state integration, accessibility
verification, or testing. **The evidence does not justify option C.** Individual Radix primitives
can be evaluated later if accessible dialogs/menus/popovers become common.

### Tailwind 4 vs 3.4

Argues **against importing v4-shaped component code**, not for bundling an app-wide Tailwind
migration into a marketing launch. Use existing 3.4 tokens; scoped CSS or CSS modules for
facade-specific work; never paste unsupported v4 utilities; keep any v4 upgrade a separate change
with before/after visual checks. Treat 21st.dev as **visual reference**, porting interaction and
composition deliberately — *"without letting a component marketplace choose the application's
architecture."*

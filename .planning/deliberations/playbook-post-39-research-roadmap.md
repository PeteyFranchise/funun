# The Playbook — Post–Release 39 Research and Exploration Roadmap

**Status:** Future research backlog · not approved release scope  
**Horizon:** After Playbook operational v1 (Releases 27–31) and only after evidence supports any enterprise-maturity work proposed for Releases 32–39  
**Decision rule:** Research does not authorize implementation. Each concept must return through product, security, privacy, legal, accessibility, and owner review before receiving a release number.

## Why this backlog exists

The Playbook can eventually become more than a document library: it may help Funūn understand organizational capability, train teams through realistic practice, preserve institutional memory, and extend governed workflows to enterprise partners. Those possibilities are valuable, but they also introduce surveillance, labor, privacy, legal, AI-governance, and complexity risks.

This document preserves the opportunities without placing them on the launch critical path. The default after Release 31 remains: activate, observe real Team Member behavior, and let evidence decide what deserves to be built.

## Research principles

- Do not score people through hidden or invasive productivity surveillance.
- Do not let AI create policy, grant authority, adjudicate employment matters, or publish doctrine.
- Use role and team capacity data to support people, not rank human worth.
- Separate personal Member Account activity from Funūn employment and enterprise administration.
- Apply least-privilege access, purpose limitation, retention controls, and auditable decisions to every new dataset.
- Preserve human appeal and correction paths whenever a system produces a recommendation, score, benchmark, or historical inference.
- Prefer interoperable, reversible extensions over dependencies that trap Funūn or its partners in one vendor.

## 1. Workforce skills and capacity forecasting

### Opportunity

Build an evidence-based view of which capabilities Funūn has, where teams are overextended, what training could close a gap, and when hiring or reassignment is healthier than increasing workload.

### Research questions

- Which skills may be self-declared, manager-validated, training-verified, or demonstrated through completed work?
- How should availability, workload, proficiency, and learning interest remain distinct signals?
- What minimum data quality is required before forecasting hiring or coverage needs?
- How can the system surface a staffing risk without turning into individual productivity surveillance?
- Which employment-law, accommodation, and collective-consultation requirements apply by jurisdiction?

### Guardrails

- No keystroke, presence-duration, private-message, or off-platform behavior tracking.
- No automated hiring, termination, promotion, compensation, or disciplinary decisions.
- Team Members can inspect and correct the data used to describe their skills.
- Capacity forecasts should default to team-level planning and reveal individual detail only where operationally necessary and authorized.

### Revisit when

Funūn has multiple functional teams, recurring coverage gaps, a stable skills vocabulary, and enough real workload history to distinguish signal from startup noise.

---

## 2. Regulatory and jurisdiction-specific policy overlays

### Opportunity

Layer approved regional guidance over a single canonical doctrine so Team Members can see what changes because of their location, the Member’s location, the buyer’s location, or the governing law of a transaction.

### Research questions

- Which facts determine an applicable overlay, and which require counsel rather than software inference?
- How should conflicts between company-wide doctrine and local requirements be resolved and displayed?
- Who owns legal review, effective dates, sunset dates, and emergency corrections?
- Can overlays be represented as controlled deltas instead of duplicating entire articles?
- What evidence must be retained to prove which guidance was effective for a person or transaction at a specific time?

### Guardrails

- Counsel-approved content only; no AI-generated legal conclusions.
- The interface must distinguish law, internal policy, guidance, and unresolved questions.
- Ambiguous jurisdiction must escalate rather than silently choosing an overlay.
- Every overlay must carry provenance, reviewer, effective date, review date, and supersession history.

### Revisit when

Funūn employs people, supports regulated workflows, or operates Client Partner relationships across jurisdictions whose requirements materially differ.

---

## 3. Advanced organizational simulations

### Opportunity

Let teams rehearse realistic incidents, rights conflicts, difficult Member situations, buyer requests, security failures, and cross-functional handoffs before those situations occur in production.

### Research questions

- Which scenarios carry the greatest operational or human risk?
- Should simulations be individual, team-based, tabletop, live, or mixed?
- How can branching decisions be evaluated without pretending one scripted answer is universally correct?
- What outcomes should update training plans, runbooks, or doctrine?
- How should psychological safety and sensitive scenario content be handled?

### Guardrails

- Simulations are learning environments, not covert performance tests.
- Participants know what is recorded, who can see it, and how long it is retained.
- High-stakes scenarios include qualified facilitators and debriefs.
- Results cannot become disciplinary evidence without a separately disclosed and lawful process.

### Revisit when

Core runbooks are stable, Release 30 training simulations have real usage, and Funūn has facilitators capable of designing and debriefing advanced exercises responsibly.

---

## 4. Benchmarking across departments

### Opportunity

Compare process health across teams to identify systemic bottlenecks, uneven service levels, useful practices, and resourcing needs.

### Research questions

- Which measures are genuinely comparable across different functions?
- How should complexity, staffing, demand, and risk be normalized?
- Which benchmarks help collaboration rather than create internal competition?
- How can qualitative Member and partner outcomes accompany quantitative measures?
- What minimum sample sizes prevent misleading comparisons?

### Guardrails

- Benchmark processes and outcomes, not personal worth.
- Never reward low reporting, premature closure, superficial volume, or other easily gamed proxies.
- Show uncertainty, context, definitions, and data limitations next to every comparison.
- Teams receive an appeal and annotation path for misleading data.

### Revisit when

Multiple departments operate the same stable workflows at sufficient volume and share agreed service definitions and ethical measurement doctrine.

## 5. Voice-based or conversational training

### Opportunity

Offer accessible, hands-free Playbook learning through spoken questions, scenario practice, role-play, summaries, and optional pronunciation or communication coaching.

### Research questions

- Which experiences require speech-to-text, text-to-speech, realtime voice, or asynchronous recordings?
- Can the experience work without storing raw voice after the session?
- How will accents, dialects, disabilities, code-switching, and multilingual speech be handled fairly?
- When must a user be told they are speaking with AI?
- How will AI spending, latency, accuracy, and provider retention be governed?

### Guardrails

- Voice is optional; equivalent text and accessible alternatives remain available.
- No emotion, personality, truthfulness, accent-quality, or protected-trait inference.
- Obtain explicit consent before recording and minimize raw-audio retention.
- AI cannot certify legal, safety, or professional competence by itself.

### Revisit when

Funūn has approved platform-wide AI entitlements and spending controls, accessible training demand, and a tested voice-data privacy standard.

---

## 6. Acquisitions and organizational-merger onboarding

### Opportunity

Help newly acquired or merged teams understand Funūn’s doctrine, map responsibilities, preserve valuable institutional knowledge, and migrate workflows without erasing local expertise.

### Research questions

- How should two policy libraries be compared, reconciled, and versioned?
- Which local practices should be preserved, retired, or escalated for leadership decision?
- How can access be staged before identity, employment, and authority records are fully reconciled?
- What information can lawfully transfer between organizations?
- How should acknowledgements and training deadlines account for transition workload?

### Guardrails

- No automatic policy overwrite or bulk authority inheritance.
- Legal, HR/TMS, privacy, security, and functional owners approve migrations in their domains.
- Preserve provenance and clearly distinguish inherited doctrine from Funūn-approved doctrine.
- Avoid using onboarding telemetry as a proxy for loyalty or culture fit.

### Revisit when

Funūn enters a real acquisition, merger, significant team transfer, or multi-entity operating structure requiring governed integration.

## 7. Anonymous doctrine-quality reporting

### Opportunity

Let Team Members identify unsafe, inaccurate, contradictory, exclusionary, or impractical guidance when normal reporting channels may feel risky.

### Research questions

- Is true anonymity technically and legally supportable, or should the product promise confidential reporting instead?
- Which metadata must be suppressed to prevent re-identification?
- Who may receive reports, investigate them, and communicate outcomes?
- How will abuse, retaliation, duplicate reports, and immediate safety threats be handled?
- Should this remain separate from formal HR, whistleblowing, ethics, or Trust and Safety channels?

### Guardrails

- Never promise anonymity if infrastructure, logs, legal process, or small-group context can identify a reporter.
- Publish clear confidentiality limits and anti-retaliation protections.
- Minimize metadata and access; maintain independent oversight for sensitive reports.
- Do not use anonymous reporting as a substitute for legally required reporting channels.

### Revisit when

TMS, Legal, Trust and Safety, and Leadership establish a formal reporting doctrine and can support protected intake, triage, investigation, appeal, and retaliation monitoring.

## 8. Enterprise partner administration

### Opportunity

Give authorized administrators at verified Client Partner organizations tools to manage their people, scoped training, organization-specific guidance, access reviews, and integration settings.

### Research questions

- Which responsibilities belong to the partner administrator versus Funūn?
- How does delegated administration interact with Member Accounts, corporate identities, and job changes?
- Which Playbook material may be shared externally, and how is accidental internal disclosure prevented?
- What enterprise controls are required for SSO, SCIM, audit exports, retention, and data residency?
- How should partner-created guidance be reviewed, labeled, isolated, and terminated?

### Guardrails

- Enterprise administration never converts personal Member Account data into employer-controlled data.
- Verified organization membership—not a self-declared professional role—grants partner capabilities.
- External users receive only explicitly published partner content; internal Playbook access stays deny-by-default.
- Organization removal and employee departure must revoke corporate access without destroying a person’s independent Member identity.

### Revisit when

Verified Client Partners request delegated administration and Funūn has mature organization membership, active-context, SSO, offboarding, and external-content isolation controls.

---

## 9. Historical decision intelligence

### Opportunity

Help authorized leaders understand why doctrine changed, what exceptions were made, which conditions shaped past decisions, and whether previous outcomes provide relevant—not binding—context for a new decision.

### Research questions

- What constitutes a comparable precedent, and who decides similarity?
- How should superseded policy, incomplete evidence, and changed circumstances be represented?
- Which decisions are too sensitive to index or summarize?
- Can the system explain its retrieval and distinguish fact from inference?
- How can retention, legal hold, privilege, and deletion requirements coexist?

### Guardrails

- Historical decisions inform; they do not automatically control new outcomes.
- Do not infer protected traits, employee risk, trustworthiness, or likely behavior.
- Preserve citations to immutable source records and expose uncertainty and changed context.
- Privileged, personnel, security, and highly sensitive records require separate access domains and may be excluded entirely.

### Revisit when

The Exception Registry, incident histories, doctrine revisions, and workflow outcomes contain enough well-governed evidence to study retrieval quality without manufacturing false precedent.

---

## 10. Custom automation and integration marketplaces

### Opportunity

Allow Funūn or approved partners to extend Playbook workflows with reusable connectors, triggers, actions, and templates while preserving authorization and auditability.

### Research questions

- Is this an internal connector registry, a curated partner marketplace, or a public developer platform?
- What permission manifest, review process, sandbox, secret storage, rate limits, and revocation model are required?
- How will marketplace items be versioned, signed, monitored, billed, and removed?
- Which actions require human confirmation or segregation of duties?
- How can workflow portability prevent vendor lock-in?

### Guardrails

- No arbitrary code in the production application or customer data plane.
- Every integration declares data access, actions, retention, subprocessors, cost, and failure behavior before installation.
- High-impact actions require explicit confirmation, idempotency, immutable receipts, and rapid revocation.
- Approved doctrine remains the authority; marketplace automation cannot create permissions or bypass required review.

### Revisit when

Release 32’s controlled Integration Hub has proven demand, stable contracts, strong observability, a secure credential model, and enough repeated integration requests to justify a platform rather than one-off connectors.

## Promotion gate for any concept

Before any item receives a release number, its research brief should establish:

1. A real user or organizational problem supported by observed evidence.
2. The smallest useful product boundary and explicit non-goals.
3. Data classification, access model, retention, correction, and deletion rules.
4. Security, privacy, employment, legal, accessibility, and AI-governance review as applicable.
5. Human decision and appeal boundaries.
6. Cost model, operational owner, support burden, and measurable success criteria.
7. A beta cohort, rollback path, and evidence required for broader release.

Until those conditions are met, these items remain research—not promises.

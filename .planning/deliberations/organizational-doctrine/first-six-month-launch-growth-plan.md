# Funūn First Six-Month Launch Growth Plan

**Status:** OWNER-APPROVED — 2026-09-07
**Starting point:** One employee, the Founder & CEO
**Time horizon:** The first six complete months after controlled launch
**Purpose:** Prove that Funūn can repeatedly help creators make and organize work, prepare rights, connect qualified music to legitimate buyers and complete trustworthy transactions before scaling headcount.

## Six-month thesis

Funūn should not attempt to look large during its first six months. It should become dependable.

The company must prove five connected loops:

1. **Creative loop:** A member can capture an idea, create a song, collaborate and return to the work without losing context.
2. **Readiness loop:** The member can progressively document people, versions, credits, rights and delivery assets without blocking creation.
3. **Buyer loop:** A legitimate buyer can find or request appropriate music, receive accurate information and move through a clear process.
4. **Transaction loop:** Funūn can preserve authority, agreement, payment, delivery and reporting evidence from beginning to end.
5. **Relationship loop:** Members and Client Partners receive useful follow-through and choose to return.

The six-month objective is not maximum signups. It is evidence that these loops work repeatedly with real people and real music.

## Planning assumptions

- Launch begins as a controlled beta with personally supported cohorts.
- “Employee” means an actual Funūn employee. Contractors, counsel, accountants and specialized vendors are reported separately.
- Revenue means Funūn net revenue. Licensing GMV and rights-holder money are separate measures.
- Numeric ranges below are operating targets for discussion, not forecasts or promises.
- Hiring is gated by workload, runway and demonstrated need. A calendar date alone never authorizes a hire.
- Product access expands only when authorization, privacy, support and recovery controls are ready for the next cohort.

## Month 1 — Establish trust and observe reality

### Primary goal

Prove that a small invited cohort can complete the core creative journey with direct founder support.

### Major achievements to unlock

- Complete production launch pre-flight for authentication, account separation, workspace authorization, storage, backups, auditability and recovery.
- Onboard the first 20–40 invited members personally.
- Observe at least 10 members create or return to a real project.
- Prove capture → save → reopen for audio, lyrics, notes and collaborators.
- Confirm that an invited collaborator enters the intended Writer's Room and can create a profile without completing rights settings first.
- Establish one support intake, one issue log and a Level 1–4 occurrence process.
- Define the first product-health, member-health, catalogue-readiness and buyer-demand dashboards.
- Conduct structured discovery with at least five prospective music buyers or Client Partner contacts.
- Record every material promise, failure and workaround uncovered during onboarding.

### Month 1 progression

#### Week 1 — Production and operating baseline

- Confirm which production environment, database, storage buckets, email providers, AI providers and third-party services are actually active.
- Review production authorization for personal member accounts, Funūn Team Member accounts, Client Partner organizations, invitations and shared workspaces.
- Verify that backups exist, determine what can be restored and perform a controlled recovery check without risking member data.
- Establish a single support address or intake form, a private incident channel and named ownership for every open production problem.
- Define the Level 1–4 occurrence model in operational language and identify who can pause invitations when a Level 1 or Level 2 issue appears.
- Capture baseline performance for sign-in, project loading, audio upload, playback, saving, invitation acceptance and mobile use.
- Prepare a short consent-based beta agreement that tells members what is being tested, how feedback is used and what is not yet promised.

**Evidence produced:** production inventory, access review, recovery record, support intake, open-risk register and beta cohort list.

#### Week 2 — Founder-led first cohort

- Onboard approximately 5–10 members through real use cases rather than scripted demonstrations.
- Ask each member to begin in the way they naturally would: voice note, beat upload, lyrics, invitation or existing song.
- Observe where the member pauses, asks for explanation, reaches for another app or abandons a task.
- Record whether the member can find the project again, understand what saved and identify who can see it.
- Ask the member to invite one real collaborator when appropriate and observe the recipient's complete acceptance journey.
- Resolve severe defects before adding the next cohort; avoid accumulating a large unprioritized suggestion list.

**Evidence produced:** session notes, reproducible defects, activation path, first-value time and member-language glossary.

#### Week 3 — Independent-use cohort

- Invite a second group without live step-by-step founder guidance.
- Give members a concise starting message and let the interface carry the experience.
- Compare independent completion with the guided cohort.
- Contact members who stop and distinguish product confusion, technical failure, lack of need and simple timing.
- Test at least one mobile upload, one mobile recording, one desktop collaboration and one cross-device return to work.
- Begin measuring meaningful activity: creating, editing, listening, collaborating or completing readiness work—not page views alone.

**Evidence produced:** guided-versus-independent comparison, device matrix, abandonment reasons and first retention signals.

#### Week 4 — Stabilize and decide

- Hold the first monthly operating review across product, security, support, member value and buyer learning, even if the founder currently holds every role.
- Close or explicitly defer the highest-impact issues with an owner and reason.
- Identify the smallest reliable creative loop and protect it from unnecessary feature additions.
- Decide whether the next cohort can safely expand, must remain the same size or should pause.
- Publish a plain-language beta update explaining what was fixed, what is being tested next and how members can report a problem.

**Month 1 learning question:** Can a real creator trust Funūn with unfinished work and successfully return to it?

### Operating targets

| Measure | Month 1 target |
|---|---:|
| Employees | 1 |
| Invited members | 20–40 |
| Members completing a real creative action | 10–20 |
| Active real projects | 10–25 |
| Buyer discovery conversations | 5–10 |
| Known unresolved Level 1 security/integrity issues | 0 at expansion gate |
| Net revenue | Validation only; $0–5K is acceptable |

### Hiring posture

Remain at one employee unless operational continuity or production safety demonstrably requires the COO earlier. Use narrowly scoped external legal, accounting, security or engineering help where qualified expertise is required.

### Month 1 gate

Do not broaden invitations until members can reliably recover their work, account context remains separated and every severe production issue has an owner and response path.

## Month 2 — Prove the complete member workflow

### Primary goal

Move from founder demonstrations to repeatable member behavior across creation, collaboration and progressive rights readiness.

### Major achievements to unlock

- Expand to 60–100 invited members in controlled cohorts.
- Establish onboarding gameplans for producers, artists, writers and mixed-role members.
- Observe members create projects without founder screen-sharing.
- Prove collaborator invitation, acceptance, room entry, role assignment and roster reconciliation.
- Prove that lyrics, audio takes, notes, comments and revisions remain recoverable across sessions and devices.
- Test split-sheet drafts and rights-information capture without interrupting songwriting.
- Identify the ten most frequent usability or support failures and close the highest-risk items.
- Prepare the first set of exact-version, evidence-backed, potentially sync-ready recordings.
- Qualify the first 2–4 Client Partner prospects and document their real workflow needs.
- Draft beta pricing hypotheses without forcing a final freemium/paid structure.

### Month 2 progression

#### Week 1 — Standardize onboarding without making it rigid

- Convert successful Month 1 onboarding patterns into reusable CRM gameplans.
- Create separate starting paths for a producer with a beat, a writer with lyrics, a vocalist with a voice note and a member bringing an existing song.
- Keep every path skippable and allow the member to enter the Writer's Room before completing PRO, IPI, publishing or rights information.
- Train the COO or designated second operator to conduct an onboarding session from the gameplan and document it in the call log.
- Compare the founder and non-founder sessions to identify undocumented knowledge.

**Evidence produced:** onboarding gameplans, call-log standard, handoff notes and list of founder-only knowledge.

#### Week 2 — Prove collaboration and identity continuity

- Run invitation tests for existing members, new members and limited guests.
- Verify that an invite accepted through one email does not silently move the person into the wrong personal or Team Member account context.
- Confirm room membership, creative participation, credit, split-sheet participation and ownership remain different facts.
- Test collaborator reconciliation so accepted invitees stop appearing perpetually “invited.”
- Test mentions, studio notes and waveform comments for creation, notification, discovery, resolution and later retrieval.
- Document every state transition and the recovery path for a mistaken invitation.

**Evidence produced:** invitation-state matrix, identity-context tests, permission findings and collaboration recovery procedures.

#### Week 3 — Add progressive readiness

- Guide members through credits, roles, IPI/CAE, PRO affiliation, publishing and SoundExchange information when relevant.
- Observe which information members know, which requires education and which requires another participant.
- Test split-sheet creation, contributor linking and document upload while explicitly preserving unfinished creative work.
- Build the first readiness queue with exact missing facts, responsible person and deadline only where a real use requires it.
- Review Lyric Lift and other AI-assisted tools for consent, cost, failure states and clear labeling.

**Evidence produced:** readiness-friction map, education needs, missing-data ownership and AI tool cost/failure baseline.

#### Week 4 — Prepare real catalogue and buyer work

- Select a small group of exact recordings whose contributors are willing to test opportunity readiness.
- Review recording versions, composition identity, contributor claims, authority evidence, metadata and delivery assets.
- Mark each version honestly as one-stop pre-cleared, pre-cleared within parameters, fast-clearance available or restricted/on hold.
- Conduct structured buyer discovery around search, briefs, clearance, delivery and cue-sheet work rather than asking only whether they “like the idea.”
- Rank pricing hypotheses by customer problem, willingness to pay, cost to serve and strategic fit.

**Month 2 learning question:** Can a member independently create and collaborate now, then add the information required for a real opportunity later?

### Hiring posture

Hire the **COO as employee #2** when founder time is being consumed by operating coordination, service recovery, vendor administration, records or internal follow-through. If that threshold is not present, preserve runway and delay the hire.

### Month 2 gate

The workflow is ready for a larger beta only when members can complete its core path without repeated founder intervention and account/workspace authorization remains dependable.

## Month 3 — Complete the first real value exchanges

### Primary goal

Demonstrate that Funūn can carry qualified music and qualified demand through an accountable commercial workflow.

### Major achievements to unlock

- Reach 125–200 invited members while monitoring cohort quality.
- Publish clear beta terms, privacy expectations, support boundaries and rights guidance.
- Open a deliberately small, reviewed Crate cohort with exact-version readiness states.
- Establish the first qualified buyer briefs or searches.
- Complete the first 1–3 real paid licences or equivalent commercial transactions where demand permits.
- Preserve buyer identity, intended use, authority, clearance, agreement, payment, delivery and reporting evidence for each transaction.
- Begin paid-member and Client Partner pricing tests with transparent beta treatment.
- Verify that Funūn net revenue, GMV and rights-holder amounts are reported separately.
- Measure time from brief to qualified response and from selection to clearance.
- Establish a weekly cross-functional operating review even if several functions are still held by the same person.

### Month 3 progression

#### Week 1 — Curate the first controlled Crate

- Admit only reviewed exact versions; do not fill the catalogue with unverified uploads.
- Confirm searchable creative metadata, rights status, contact route, clearance parameters and delivery asset for every admitted version.
- Show restrictions and approval requirements internally before a track can reach a buyer.
- Test search results against several real briefs and record false matches, missing matches and metadata gaps.
- Confirm that removing or holding a version immediately prevents new unauthorized use while preserving history.

**Evidence produced:** Crate admission record, readiness distribution, search-quality findings and hold/removal test.

#### Week 2 — Operate the first qualified brief

- Record the buyer, production, decision-maker, authority, use, territory, term, media, budget, deadline and confidentiality restrictions.
- Assign one relationship owner and one deal operator, even if the same employee temporarily fills both roles.
- Produce a short list based on fit and eligibility rather than volume.
- Record why each work was considered, submitted, withheld or rejected.
- Give the buyer one coordinated response and give members only the information they are authorized to receive.

**Evidence produced:** normalized brief, consideration record, shortlist rationale, communication history and service timing.

#### Week 3 — Complete the commercial control path

- Confirm the exact recording and composition selected.
- Collect all required approvals and make unresolved or conditional authority visible.
- Present approved commercial terms and the correct agreement.
- Capture signatures, payment status and delivery authorization separately.
- Deliver only the approved asset and preserve a receipt of what was delivered, to whom and under which licence.
- Create cue-sheet and post-use follow-up tasks at the time of delivery.

**Evidence produced:** complete transaction record from request through authorized delivery.

#### Week 4 — Review the first transactions and pricing

- Conduct a transaction postmortem with the buyer-facing, member-facing, rights, contract, finance and technical perspectives represented.
- Calculate gross transaction value, rights-holder obligations, Funūn net revenue, direct service cost and gross profit separately.
- Identify every manual step, unclear approval, duplicated entry and avoidable delay.
- Interview both sides about trust, clarity, speed and willingness to return.
- Revise pricing hypotheses without hiding beta limitations or subsidized founder labor.

**Month 3 learning question:** Can Funūn complete a defensible commercial transaction—not merely produce a promising introduction?

## Month 4 — Make success repeatable

### Primary goal

Reduce founder-dependent execution and show that different members and buyers can complete the same value loops.

### Major achievements to unlock

- Expand to 225–350 members only if support and reliability remain healthy.
- Complete multiple onboarding cohorts using the same approved gameplans.
- Demonstrate repeat creative activity rather than one-time project creation.
- Maintain a clearly labeled Crate population with current eligibility and automatic holds for expired or changed evidence.
- Complete transactions for more than one buyer organization and more than one rights-holder group.
- Earn at least one repeat brief, repeat search or second transaction from an existing buyer.
- Establish member-visible Funūn Now communication for available features, current focus, coming-soon work and important updates.
- Launch basic usage and spending controls for AI-assisted tools before broader exposure.
- Test support escalation, production incident response and restore/recovery procedures.
- Convert recurring manual work into documented procedures or product requirements.

### Month 4 progression

#### Week 1 — Expand through cohorts, not an open floodgate

- Invite the next member cohort through identifiable sources so activation and retention can be compared.
- Set a maximum cohort size based on current onboarding and support capacity.
- Use automated onboarding only where Month 1–3 evidence shows it improves understanding.
- Preserve a human route for identity, access, rights, safety and payment problems.
- Review cohort health before sending the next invitation group.

**Evidence produced:** source-based cohort report, support capacity measure and expansion decision record.

#### Week 2 — Remove founder bottlenecks

- Assign recurring operating work to the COO, Product/Technology leader or documented external provider.
- Test whether another authorized person can reconstruct the status of a member, buyer, project, brief or transaction from the system of record.
- Convert repeated founder decisions into doctrine, delegated limits or explicit escalation rules.
- Remove shared credentials and undocumented access accumulated during launch.
- Establish coverage for urgent support and incidents when the founder is unavailable.

**Evidence produced:** delegation map, access cleanup, coverage schedule and founder-dependency register.

#### Week 3 — Test recovery and control under load

- Run a controlled support surge or cohort day to observe queue handling.
- Recheck storage, signed URLs, authorization boundaries, notification delivery and account switching under realistic use.
- Exercise backup recovery, incident communication and rollback responsibilities.
- Confirm that AI tools have per-account attribution, failure handling and spending controls.
- Measure slow pages, failed jobs, abandoned uploads and repeated support contacts.

**Evidence produced:** load observations, recovery exercise, AI cost controls and reliability backlog.

#### Week 4 — Prove buyer repetition

- Ask previous buyers for the next genuine brief or workflow need rather than a testimonial alone.
- Determine whether the buyer returns for catalogue, workflow, service, relationship or price.
- Complete another transaction through the revised process and compare effort and cycle time.
- Identify the smallest repeatable Client Partner package and the promises Funūn can consistently keep.
- Feed buyer demand patterns to A&R and catalogue planning without converting them into guarantees for members.

**Month 4 learning question:** Does the system become easier and more trustworthy the second time it is used?

## Month 5 — Validate pricing, retention and service economics

### Primary goal

Learn which customers repeatedly receive enough value to pay, what it costs Funūn to serve them and which workflows should remain high-touch.

### Major achievements to unlock

- Run explicit paid tests for member subscriptions, Client Partner access or workflow products and Funūn's transaction economics.
- Track conversion, retention, support burden, AI cost and gross profit by customer type.
- Establish service boundaries so optional help does not imply legal management, guaranteed opportunity or compulsory administration.
- Introduce the first reusable buyer and member SLAs.
- Maintain opportunity distribution records sufficient to examine relevance and fairness.
- Prove that another Team Member can onboard, follow up or operate a workflow using The Playbook and CRM record.
- Establish structured catalogue, metadata and verification queues.
- Conduct the first monthly risk, access and financial-control review.
- Decide which beta features remain free, which require limits and which are candidates for post-beta paid tiers.

### Month 5 progression

#### Week 1 — Run explicit pricing cohorts

- Offer clearly described paid beta choices to defined member and Client Partner cohorts.
- State what is included, what has usage limits, what remains experimental and what can be cancelled.
- Keep AI-assisted features visible in cost reporting even when temporarily included at no charge.
- Record why a customer accepts, declines, downgrades or requests a different arrangement.
- Avoid permanent “lifetime” promises before cost and retention behavior are understood.

**Evidence produced:** pricing-cohort matrix, conversion reasons, objections and feature-cost attribution.

#### Week 2 — Calculate service economics

- Measure employee and contractor time spent on onboarding, support, clearance, contracts, catalogue review and each transaction.
- Calculate contribution margin by product and customer type.
- Identify workflows that should be automated, remain premium high-touch services or be discontinued.
- Test whether the price reflects the value and cost without depending on rights-holder money.
- Create an account-wide AI spending view and provisional ceilings before broader paid access.

**Evidence produced:** unit-economics model, service-time study, AI spend report and margin risks.

#### Week 3 — Prove operational delegation

- Have non-founder owners conduct onboarding, support, catalogue review and commercial coordination within explicit permissions.
- Audit a sample of their records for completeness, tone, authority and privacy.
- Use Playbook material during real work and capture where doctrine is missing or hard to apply.
- Confirm that training completion does not automatically provide sensitive access or approval authority.
- Review whether workload justifies A&R, Music/Creative Operations or another specialist hire.

**Evidence produced:** delegated-work audit, training gaps, authority exceptions and hiring trigger evidence.

#### Week 4 — Conduct the first integrated control review

- Reconcile member counts, projects, catalogue versions, buyer organizations, transactions, invoices, cash and participant obligations.
- Review privileged access, dormant accounts, vendor credentials and support access.
- Review open rights conflicts, Trust and Safety matters, payment exceptions and unresolved Level 1–2 occurrences.
- Compare promised SLAs with actual performance and adjust staffing or promises honestly.
- Approve the Month 6 evidence plan and assign every required measure to an owner.

**Month 5 learning question:** Which parts of Funūn create repeatable value with sustainable economics and trustworthy service?

## Month 6 — Reach a defensible operating foundation

### Primary goal

Finish the launch period with evidence that Funūn is useful, trusted, commercially promising and operationally ready for the next hiring stage.

### Major achievements to unlock

- Complete a six-month product, security, authorization, financial and doctrine review.
- Identify the strongest retained member cohorts and repeatable Client Partner use cases.
- Demonstrate multiple end-to-end transactions with accurate rights, agreements, payments, delivery and follow-up.
- Establish one accountable owner and one current status for every active member relationship, buyer relationship, brief, transaction and severe issue.
- Maintain a reviewed Crate with exact-version readiness rather than a large unverified catalogue.
- Establish monthly cohort retention, account retention, net revenue, gross profit and cash reporting.
- Publish the first approved internal training paths and require explicit authority for sensitive functions.
- Produce the next 12-month operating plan from observed demand rather than the original assumptions.
- Decide whether Funūn has earned broader launch, another controlled beta cohort or a deliberate period of reliability work.

### Month 6 progression

#### Week 1 — Audit the six-month evidence

- Reconcile every headline metric to a defined source and remove duplicates, demos and internal test records.
- Segment members by intended use, acquisition source, activation behavior, retention and payment—not by a single undifferentiated total.
- Segment buyers by verified organization, transaction-only access, workflow used, repeat behavior and revenue quality.
- Review a sample of projects and transactions from beginning to end for identity, authorization, evidence and financial integrity.
- List assumptions that remain unproven rather than turning them into facts through repetition.

**Evidence produced:** reconciled scorecard, cohort analysis, transaction audit and open-assumption register.

#### Week 2 — Stress-test the operating model

- Have the founder step out of selected daily workflows and observe whether documented owners can continue them.
- Run a tabletop Level 1 incident involving account compromise, unreleased audio exposure, rights-integrity failure or payment risk.
- Test escalation from Support to Product, Rights, Legal, Finance, TMS or Trust and Safety.
- Confirm that member and Client Partner communications remain coherent during the exercise.
- Identify the maximum safe cohort, catalogue and buyer volume the current team can support.

**Evidence produced:** continuity test, incident exercise, escalation findings and current capacity ceiling.

#### Week 3 — Build the evidence-based next-year plan

- Select the strongest member segments and buyer use cases based on retention, willingness to pay and strategic fit.
- Choose the product investments that remove the binding constraint in each proven loop.
- Build a 12-month hiring plan using workload triggers and runway, beginning with the approved first-ten sequence.
- Create base, constrained and accelerated financial scenarios.
- Define the next two quarters' member, catalogue, Client Partner, transaction, reliability and revenue goals.
- Identify which promises Funūn will not make in the next stage.

**Evidence produced:** 12-month operating plan, hiring gates, financial scenarios and next-stage product priorities.

#### Week 4 — Decide and communicate

- Hold a formal Leadership review and choose Expand, Deepen or Reposition.
- Record the evidence, dissent, risks, owner, review date and conditions that would change the decision.
- Tell Team Members what changes, what remains stable and which metrics matter next.
- Tell members and Client Partners what Funūn learned, what is available and what will happen next without presenting plans as shipped features.
- Close the launch period with explicit ownership for every remaining issue and commitment.

**Month 6 learning question:** Has Funūn become a repeatable operating system, or is it still a set of founder-led exceptions?

## The Month 6 decision

At the six-month review, Leadership chooses one of three honest paths:

### 1. Expand

Choose expansion when member retention, buyer repetition, transaction integrity, service quality and gross-profit evidence are all credible. Begin hiring toward the first-ten plan and broaden access in controlled cohorts.

### 2. Deepen

Choose a reliability and product-depth period when users value Funūn but important workflows remain too manual, fragile or confusing. Preserve the strongest cohort and fix the constraints before adding acquisition expense.

### 3. Reposition

Choose repositioning when one customer group or workflow is clearly more valuable than the original model. Preserve the mission and evidence, but change product emphasis, pricing or go-to-market deliberately.

Failure to hit a signup number is not automatically failure. Concealing weak retention, unsafe rights data, unprofitable service or unreliable product behavior would be.

## Six-month scorecard

Leadership should review five scorecard areas together:

| Area | Core question |
|---|---|
| Member value | Are intended members returning to create, organize and advance real work? |
| Buyer value | Are legitimate buyers returning with qualified needs and completing useful workflows? |
| Transaction integrity | Can Funūn prove authority, agreement, payment, delivery and reporting for every transaction? |
| Economic quality | Is net revenue recurring or repeatable, gross profit improving and service cost understood? |
| Organizational readiness | Can the work continue through documented systems without relying entirely on the founder's memory? |

## Anti-gaming rules

- Do not count invited accounts as active members.
- Do not count uploaded files as sync-ready exact versions.
- Do not count a pitch as a licence or a verbal interest as contracted revenue.
- Do not count licensing GMV as Funūn revenue.
- Do not count rights-holder money as operating cash.
- Do not count a handoff until the receiving owner accepts it.
- Do not hide reopened support cases or unresolved incidents to improve closure metrics.
- Do not expand access while known critical authorization or data-integrity failures remain unresolved.
- Do not hire to reach a headcount target when the workload, runway or leadership capacity is absent.

## Governing launch principle

> In the first six months, Funūn should earn the right to grow by proving that real creative work, real relationships and real transactions can move through the platform safely and repeatedly.

<objective>
Append the ownership and member-transition RPCs to migration 198 — the transactional half of R-05
(WSR-07), R-22 (WSR-08) and F15 (WSR-11).

**Three defects close here.**

*F15 — the owner floor is counted in a different transaction from the write.*
`refuseIfOwnerFloorBreaks` counts owners in one transaction and the UPDATE happens in another, so
two concurrent demotions of two different owners each see one remaining owner and both proceed.
The trigger catches it today — which is why this is correctness-preserved-but-ugly, surfaced as a
409 derived from **string-matching an English error message**. Counting after the lock, in the write
transaction, makes the trigger the backstop it was meant to be rather than the primary control.

*F5 / WSR-07 — nothing actor-relative exists.* Migration 197's new trigger enforces the structural
half (a row may not become owner, an owner row may not change, outside the definer path). The
actor-relative half — only owners may promote, no self-promotion, admins may not touch owner rows —
can only be checked where the actor is known, which is inside these RPCs against `p_actor_id`.

*WSR-08 — there is no two-sided ownership transfer at all.* Migration 197 created the diary table;
this plan writes the two RPCs that use it.

**The write-order rule, stated again because getting it backwards raises 42501.**
`guard_workspace_never_zero_owners` runs inside your transaction and sees your own uncommitted
writes from *previous statements*. Promote the successor in statement 1; demote the incumbent in
statement 2. In that order the trigger firing on the demotion counts the freshly-promoted successor
and passes.

Purpose: make the workspace's own authority hierarchy enforceable, atomically, with every refusal
that matters on the record.

Output: three RPCs appended to migration 198, and the matching describe blocks in its suite.
